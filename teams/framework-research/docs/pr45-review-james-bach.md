## The story of how this passes review and still ships a bug

A junior engineer is handed an APP-7 strangler wave over a legacy APEX-era pricing module. They follow [PR #45](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/pull/45) to the letter. They write a characterization test: feed the legacy module its real inputs, capture what it returns, assert that. Green. They strangle the module behind the gateway; the new code reproduces the captured output exactly. Green. Verifier ≠ Author signs off — the diff is small. CI is green. Per ENG-7 the migration is "reversible," per ENG-1 the behavior is "pinned." Everyone did everything the document asks.

And they shipped the bug. Because the legacy module rounded VAT the wrong way for fifteen years, and the characterization test **pinned the bug as the spec.** The green checkmark now actively *defends* the defect — anyone who later fixes the rounding breaks "passing" tests and gets told they regressed.

**This is the central finding, and it is paired-consensus with Beck:** a test can trace cleanly to a human-authored Given/When/Then (Beck happy) and still assert behavior that is *wrong* (Bach unhappy). The PR's own framing makes it worse — [CODING_STANDARDS.md](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/blob/master/CODING_STANDARDS.md) and [WORKFLOW.md](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/blob/master/WORKFLOW.md) both say characterization tests "capture current observable behavior," and call them a "regression net that makes a strangler wave reversible." **Nowhere does the wording acknowledge that the pinned behavior may itself be a bug, nor tell the engineer what to do when it is.** ENG-7 in [ADR-012](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-012-eng-cd-trunk-review.md) has the same silence.

### F1 — Characterization tests pin bugs; the guidance never says so (consensus w/ Beck)
The three edits describe characterization tests as a pure good. The missing sentence: *"A characterization test pins current behavior including any defects in it; before pinning, judge whether the captured behavior is correct or merely current, and where it is wrong, write the corrected expectation as the target and record the divergence."* Without that, the process manufactures confidence that the migration preserved *correctness* when it only preserved *output.* Add it to [WORKFLOW.md](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/blob/master/WORKFLOW.md) and ENG-7.

## SFDPOT applied to the process change itself

**Data + Time — the captured behavior may not be a fixed thing to capture.**
"Pin current observable behavior" assumes the legacy module is a deterministic function. The hard legacy cases aren't: behavior that depends on `Date.now()`, on row order from an unindexed query, on locale/timezone, on a nightly batch's prior state, on floating-point accumulation. Capture it Tuesday, replay it Wednesday, the assertion flaps — or worse, it's pinned to one accidental sample and the *other* legacy behaviors silently aren't covered at all. The PR says "characterization tests before changing legacy" with zero acknowledgment that **non-deterministic or time-dependent legacy is the normal case in an APEX/Oracle estate**, and that capturing it requires controlling the clock, the seed, the data fixture. [GAP] No EVR doc on Workers/edge time behavior (cold-start clock skew, retry-induced duplicate execution, request replay) in [Arhitecture](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture) — so the Time hazard at the *edge* boundary, where these strangled modules will actually run, is entirely unaddressed by any EVR source. **Finding F2:** add a determinism caveat — characterization tests of non-deterministic legacy must pin the *seam* (clock, RNG, data) before they pin the behavior, or they pin noise.

**Operations — "small enough to review meaningfully" has a hole you can drive a truck through.**
The control is *size.* The failure mode is *semantic density.* A 30-line diff that rewires a money-rounding rule, a tax-jurisdiction lookup, and a retry policy is "small" by every line-count instinct a tired reviewer has — and is far less reviewable than a 600-line rename. The PR makes size the proxy for reviewability ("split if not," "too large to review honestly gets rubber-stamped"). **Finding F3:** the real control is *cognitive load / blast radius,* not line count. A reviewer rubber-stamps a small dense diff *with more confidence* than a large trivial one, because it *looks* reviewed. State that small-and-dense is the trap, not just large-and-sprawling. [ADR-012](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-012-eng-cd-trunk-review.md) ENG-6's "sized to review meaningfully" is better worded than the PR's "small enough" — port the ADR's *meaningfully* and define it as blast-radius, not length.

**The illusion-of-quality angle — this is what worries me most about the PR as a whole.**
[PR #45](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/pull/45) adds *checklist items.* Checklists produce the feeling of rigor. A box that says "[ ] Current behavior pinned by characterization tests" gets ticked when *a* characterization test exists — not when the engineer has *investigated whether the pinned behavior is correct, whether it's deterministic, whether the test covers the behaviors that matter rather than the one happy sample.* **Finding F4:** every item here can be honestly ticked by someone who did the ceremony and skipped the thinking. The ENG-7 box especially converts an act of *investigation* (is this behavior right? is it stable? what did I fail to capture?) into an act of *compliance* (did I write a test? yes). That is the precise mechanism by which adding rigor-shaped checkboxes can *reduce* actual quality — the box substitutes for the inquiry. The fix is not more boxes; it's wording on the existing ones that names the judgment the box cannot enforce (F1's missing sentence does double duty here).

## On ENG-8 / [ADR-015](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-015-contract-testing.md) (contract testing) — adjacent, and a gap in #45
[ADR-015](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-015-contract-testing.md)'s own line — *"a contract you don't test is a comment"* — is exactly the illusion-of-quality risk pointed at contracts. Note that **[PR #45](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/pull/45) reflects ENG-7 (characterization) and ENG-6 (review) into [dev-toolkit](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit) but does NOT add any ENG-8 contract-test step.** A strangler wave (the very scenario the new characterization wording targets) replaces a module *behind a gateway seam* — which is precisely where ENG-8 says the consumer-driven contract test must guard. So the PR tells the engineer to pin the legacy module's *internal* behavior (ENG-7) but is silent on verifying the *seam contract* with its live consumers (ENG-8) across the swap. **Finding F5:** the characterization net catches "did the module's output change"; it does not catch "did I break a consumer who depended on the old contract." Those are different failures. [#45](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/pull/45) should cross-reference the ENG-8 contract-test step for APP-7 waves, or it teaches half the safety net.

## Summary of findings
- **F1 (consensus w/ Beck, highest priority):** Guidance never admits a characterization test pins bugs-as-spec; add the missing sentence + a "is this behavior correct or merely current?" judgment step. Same silence in ENG-7.
- **F2:** No determinism/time caveat — non-deterministic & time-dependent legacy (the normal case) needs the clock/RNG/data seam pinned first. [GAP] edge-Time failure modes unsourced.
- **F3:** "Small enough to review" uses line-count as proxy; real control is semantic density / blast radius. Small-and-dense is the rubber-stamp trap.
- **F4 (illusion of quality):** Checkboxes convert investigation into compliance; each item is honestly tickable without the thinking it implies. Fix by wording, not more boxes.
- **F5:** [#45](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/dev-toolkit/pull/45) reflects ENG-7 but omits ENG-8 ([ADR-015](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-015-contract-testing.md)) contract-tests at the gateway seam — half the strangler safety net.

Test-rhythm / TDD-cadence judgments I defer to Beck per our split. The fidelity of the wording to [ADR-012](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-012-eng-cd-trunk-review.md)/[ADR-015](https://github.com/Eesti-Raudtee/Arhitecture/blob/master/principles/adr-015-contract-testing.md) is sound; my findings are about what the wording, faithfully followed, still fails to catch.
