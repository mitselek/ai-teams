## TLDR

When an author queries a wiki entry they themselves authored — not as a reread of their own draft, but as a real lookup against canon during independent work — that query is recursive validation by query, structurally distinct from first-use-recursive-validation (validation by application). A canon entry queried by its own author as canon is load-bearing-by-query.

## Key ideas

- **Three preconditions**: the author wrote the canon, the query is independent of the canon-creation moment, the query is the working query (not a self-test).
- **Recursive-validation family, two axes**: mechanism (application vs citation vs re-derivation) × what-is-validated (rule effectiveness vs canon indexability vs structural soundness).
- **Validates canon indexability**: is the canon retrievable and applicable when queried as canon, not just internally consistent at authorship time?
- **NOT a two-consumer-pattern instance**: that names a different-access bridge; this names same-access temporal independence (author-as-consumer has the same access as anyone).
- **Author-as-consumer is the easiest case for the canon to pass** — failure there almost certainly means failure for non-author consumers, detectable at zero coordination cost.
- **First instance**: Monte→T04→Cal→Monte citation chain (two recursion layers, both validating indexability).
- **n=1 watch**; promotion-grade-at-n=1 by joint-cross-specialist criterion. Filing-and-amendment lifecycle is itself Instance 5 of relay-fidelity (verbatim prose unrecoverable; structural framing recovered via author-scratchpad).
