## TLDR

In deeply-aligned dyads (two agents in a shared frame), crossed messages are a healthy-velocity signal of mutual prediction-accuracy, not miscommunication. Reconcile by mapping the convergence rather than re-litigating the framing. The asymmetric-cross 3-vector framework names which routing modes cross at which rates, distinguishing healthy-velocity crosses from routing-latency artifacts.

## Key ideas

- **DYAD precondition is deeply-shared frame**: both ends predicted the other's content; they cross because both are ahead of routing latency.
- **Reconcile by mapping convergence**: surface the cross, name the convergence-point, advance — no re-derivation, no content-drift.
- **Counter-pattern**: dyads NOT in shared frame produce content-drift (the original cross-in-flight gotcha).
- **Three routing vectors**: (i) direct dyad-to-dyad (~30-90s, low cross-rate), (ii) coordinator-relayed/Aen-relay (~5-15min, elevated), (iii) author-direct-DM (~30-90s, reliable).
- **Routing-mode is a load-bearing latency variable**, not uniform substrate; S36 confirmed (ii)-vector 0 arrivals vs (iii)-vector 4/5 in same Stage 2 burst.
- **Two recovery mechanisms**: team-lead intervention at ~5+ passes, dyad-side fast-forward maps at ~3+ passes.
- **n=11+ instances S36-S37** — strongest empirical base of any S36-S37 candidate; cross-team confirmation promotes to confidence-high.
