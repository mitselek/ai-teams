## TLDR

The framework's single-provider advantage is ultimately about knowledge coherence — all agents producing, classifying, and consuming knowledge in semantically compatible ways — not about infrastructure lock-in. Infrastructure lock-in is a platform choice; knowledge coherence is a model behavioral property that single-provider guarantees and multi-provider puts at risk.

## Key ideas

- **Independent convergence**: six agents analyzed the single-provider question from six domains; every response converged on the knowledge layer as the binding constraint (Brunel/peers-contribute-state, Herald/protocol-interpretation, Celes/behavioral-compliance, Finn/prompt-portability, Montesquieu/governance-scaling, Cal/classification-quality).
- **Reframing**: not "which roles tolerate different providers?" but "which knowledge FLOWS tolerate different providers?" — roles are a proxy; flows are the constraint.
- **Safe for multi-provider**: roles that don't submit to or read from the knowledge layer (RED, GREEN, Eilama-class).
- **Risky**: any role that submits to the Librarian, flags staleness, classifies confidence, or curates wiki.
- **Observation, not authoritative** (Monte's three rules): cites the topic files observed; promotion needs topic-owner review; not a substitute for reading the sources.
