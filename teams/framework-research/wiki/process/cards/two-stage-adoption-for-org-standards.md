## TLDR

When the publisher of a standard does not have create-permission in the canonical destination space, route adoption through explicit stages. Work proceeds at full quality before formal acceptance, while preserving authoritative-space ownership.

## Key ideas

- **Stage 0 — proposal**: publish in your own permitted space with a banner ("Ettepanek — ootab vastu võtmist"); work is real and complete, the banner is the only thing distinguishing it from v1.0.
- **Stage 1 — escalation**: open a tracking issue in the canonical-space owner's queue asking them to move the page, review, escalate, identify the receiving subteam. The issue is the formal handoff signal.
- **Stage 2 — final**: owner accepts → banner removed, v1.0, page moves to authoritative space, issue closed. Content is identical; the shift is location and authority, not substance.
- **Decouples three concerns conventional adoption conflates**: authoring (Stage 0), ownership transfer (Stage 1), authority (Stage 2). Conflating produces two-source-of-truth problems or indefinite draft limbo.
- **Three conditions to use**: publisher lacks create-permission, publisher has the expertise (owner doesn't or lacks bandwidth), document must land authoritatively eventually.
- **Pairs with create-perm-as-404-disguise** (the constraint making Stage 0 necessary).
- **First instance**: EVR sisene konteinerite standard (S22, TPS-583).
